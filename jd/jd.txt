Key Responsibilities

Python Engineer
Description

Department: Engineering (Data Platform Engineering)

Why This Role Exists

We operate a multi-tenant automotive SaaS platform serving thousands of dealer groups across 

the United States, running on a native AWS (NAWS) backend — Lambda, EventBridge, 

DynamoDB, S3, Step Functions.

That platform doesn't operate alone. It sits between dealer groups and the systems they already 

run — DMS platforms (CDK, Reynolds & Reynolds, Tekion, DealerTrack, Procede), CRMs, 

digital retail tools, F&I providers, and lenders. Over 40 partner platforms feed or consume our 

data today, and every one of them has its own auth model, its own schema, its own idea of what 

a "deal" is, and its own opinion about how much traffic you're allowed to send.

That integration surface is the product. If a dealer's data doesn't land clean and on time, nothing 

downstream works — not the desking tool, not the reporting, not the AI. This role owns that 

surface end to end: you are the engineer who reads the partner's spec, gets on the call with their 

team when the spec is wrong, builds the ingestion in Python on AWS, and keeps it healthy in 

production.

The engineering is only half of it. The other half is the partner's integration team: reading 

between the lines of their documentation, getting a straight answer about undocumented 

behavior, and pushing back on a bad contract before it becomes our problem. We want an 

engineer who does that work well and understands what dealer workflow the data actually serves 

— not one who moves JSON from A to B and calls it done.

Scope & Scale

• Over 40 integrated partner platforms across DMS, CRM, Digital Retail, F&I, and lending 

— each with distinct auth, rate limits, and data models.

• 5,000+ dealer tenants, each with isolated data and per-tenant partner configuration.

• Tens of thousands of API requests daily across REST, SOAP, webhook, and file-based 

integration surfaces.

• Partner outages, silent schema changes, and rate-limit shifts are routine, not exceptional 

— the integrations you own have to survive them without a human in the loop.

What You'll Own

• End-to-end partner integrations — from spec review and sandbox access through 

production cutover. You write the Python, define the mapping, and own the result.

• The partner-facing technical relationship — you're the engineer on the call with their 

integration team: clarifying undocumented behavior, negotiating payload changes, 

driving certification, and escalating when their sandbox is down.

• Ingestion and normalization pipelines — webhook receivers, polling clients, and file based feeds landing into S3 and promoting through the medallion layers into the 

normalized model.

• Resilience under someone else's SLA — idempotency and deduplication, exponential 

backoff, rate-limit handling, replay of missed events, and graceful degradation when a 

partner goes dark.

• Integration security — webhook signature verification, OAuth2/JWT credential 

lifecycles, KMS encryption, and credential storage in SSM/Secrets Manager. No secrets 

in code, ever.

• Contract testing and validation — JSON Schema validation at the boundary, contract 

tests against partner sandboxes, and CI gates that catch a partner's breaking change before 

a dealer does.

• Observability and integration health — CloudWatch dashboards, alarms, and 

structured logging per partner, so a 2 AM failure is diagnosable without guessing.

• Documentation that survives you — mapping specs, runbooks, and onboarding guides 

per partner. The next engineer should not have to re-derive what you learned on a call.

What You Bring

Must Have

• 10+ years of software engineering experience with strong, current Python.

• Judgment about scope: you start from what the integration is worth to the dealer, not from 

the most interesting way to build it — and you've shipped the boring version when the 

boring version was right.

• Hands-on production experience integrating third-party APIs you don't control —

including at least one integration you owned through spec, build, certification, and 

production support.

• Comfort across integration styles, not just modern REST: SOAP/XML, webhooks, and 

file-based feeds are all in scope here.

• Hands-on AWS experience (Lambda, S3, EventBridge, DynamoDB, Step Functions, 

Secrets Manager/SSM).

• Real experience mapping and normalizing inconsistent external schemas into a canonical 

model — and the judgment to know when the partner's data is simply wrong.

• Ability to debug distributed, event-driven systems from logs and traces.

• Willingness to be the person on the partner call. Direct, clear, and unbothered by pushing 

back on someone else's engineering team.

Strongly Preferred

• Idempotency and deduplication patterns for at-least-once delivery (e.g. DynamoDB backed dedup).

• Webhook security: HMAC signature verification, mTLS, replay protection.

• OAuth2 / JWT credential flows and rotation for machine-to-machine integrations.

• JSON Schema or comparable contract validation at system boundaries.

• Infrastructure as code (CDK) and CI/CD (CircleCI).

Nice to Have

• Automotive retail, DMS/CRM, fintech, or multi-tenant marketplace platform experience.

• Experience standing up a partner integration program from scratch (templates, 

scorecards, onboarding runbooks).

Functions, SQS; REST, SOAP/XML, webhook, and SFTP surfaces.

• Cloud Services (NAWS — native AWS stack): Lambda, S3, DynamoDB, EventBridge,

• Languages: Modern Python.

• Data Platform: S3 data lake with medallion layering (landing → bronze → silver →

• Infrastructure & Security: CDK, IAM, Auth0, OpenTelemetry, CircleCI.

How You'll Use AI

This is not a "we are AI-curious" company. We use Claude every day, and we expect you to as 

well. Concrete examples for this role:

• Turning vendor API docs, WSDLs, and sample payloads into typed Python client 

skeletons and mapping code.

• Using Claude Code to scaffold Lambda handlers, JSON Schemas, and unit tests from a 

partner spec.

• Diffing a partner's new spec version against the old one to find the breaking change fast.

• Drafting integration runbooks and mapping documentation from the code you just wrote.

• Reasoning over production logs to isolate whether a failure is ours or theirs.

You will not be replaced by AI. You will be the engineer who onboards partners at a pace the 

rest of the industry can't match.
